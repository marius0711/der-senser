# Threaded joints

## Q: What is the difference between a hard and a soft joint? ^as-001
DE: Was ist der Unterschied zwischen einem harten und einem weichen Schraubfall?
Defined by the rotation angle from snug (head contact) to final torque (ISO 5393):

- **Hard joint**: final torque reached within about **30°**, e.g. metal on metal.
- **Soft joint**: needs about **720°** or more, e.g. gaskets, springs, plastic parts.

Joint type drives tool choice and the tightening strategy.

## Q: Why is torque-angle tightening often preferred over pure torque control? ^as-002
DE: Warum wird drehmoment- und drehwinkelgesteuertes Anziehen oft dem rein drehmomentgesteuerten Anziehen vorgezogen?
With pure torque control, roughly **90 %** of the torque is used to overcome friction
(about 50 % under the head, 40 % in the thread). Only around 10 % creates clamp load, so
friction scatter translates directly into preload scatter.

Monitoring or controlling the angle makes the preload more consistent and detects
errors such as cross-threading, missing washers or wrong screws.

## Term: Vorspannkraft = preload (clamp load) ^as-003
The axial force a tightened screw exerts on the joint. It is the actual goal of tightening; torque is only an indirect means.
